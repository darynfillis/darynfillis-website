STEP 01  ·  CHECK — WHAT YOU'RE CHECKING AGAINST

Three dates. Know all three.

Mar 18, 2026
RULES ISSUED
Fannie LL-2026-03 · Freddie Bulletin 2026-C. Transition period — some lenders are already applying the new rules. That inconsistency is itself a reason to check.
Aug 3, 2026
LIMITED REVIEW DIES
The key date. Full Review on every conventional loan in a project over ten units. Three weeks away.
Jan 4, 2027
RESERVE RULE
Reserve funding minimum goes 10% → 15% of the HOA's annual budget. A 50% increase in the required allocation. Buildings at the old floor fall off.
!

Already in effect: the critical-repairs rule. Unaddressed safety, structural, or habitability issues make a project ineligible — not "subject to additional review." Ineligible. Mold, water intrusion, advanced deterioration, a failed inspection, or unfunded repairs over $10,000/unit due within 12 months.

CRITICAL REPAIRS  ·  THE PART THAT SAVES DEALS

Funding is what removes the issue.

Everyone fixates on the $10,000-per-unit number. That number is real — but it has carve-outs that agents almost never know about, and they are the difference between a dead listing and a closed one.

TRIGGERS THE RULE

  • Mold, water intrusion, or damaging leaks
  • Advanced physical deterioration
  • A deficiency that could fail within 12 months
  • A failed state/county/city safety inspection — an open SB 326 finding lands here
  • Unfunded repairs > $10,000/unit due within 12 months

DOES NOT TRIGGER IT

  • Routine and preventive maintenance
  • Normal capital replacement on schedule
  • Repairs an individual owner is paying for
  • Repairs funded by a special assessment
  • Damage isolated to a few units that doesn't affect the project's safety or habitability

Read that again: a special assessment is a carve-out, not a red flag. A $30,000-per-unit waterproofing project that's unfunded kills the building. The same project, funded by a voted assessment, is warrantable. Same repair. Different answer. That's a conversation you can start with an HOA board before you list.

THE CALIFORNIA-SPECIFIC LAYER

SB 326. And the myth about the extension.

Who it hits
HOAs with 3+ units and wood-supported balconies, decks, walkways, or stairways. That's most older Southern California condo stock.
The deadline
First inspection was due January 1, 2025. That is now more than eighteen months past. Every nine years after.
AB 2579
The extension does not apply to condos. It extended SB 721 — rental apartments. If an HOA manager told your seller they have until 2027, they conflated the two laws. Condos had no extension.
The financing link
An open SB 326 finding is a failed safety inspection — which drops it straight into the critical-repairs rule. That's the whole building, ineligible.

Watch for this: "The inspection was done" tells you nothing about whether deficiencies were found or addressed. Ask for the actual report — not a one-line summary from the listing agent. Typical timeline from contracting to certificate: four to eight weeks. That's a timeline you can only manage if you know about it before you list.

STEP 02  ·  CLEAR

The nine red flags that kill condo deals.

Almost every non-warrantable project fails for one of these. Knowing which one applies tells you exactly which conversation to have.

01
Active litigation

HOA as a party. Construction defect, slip-and-fall, vendor disputes. Neighbor-vs-neighbor generally doesn't count. Ask directly, get it in writing.

02
Pending special assessment

Voted or announced, collection or not. But remember — if it funds a critical repair, it can move the project back to warrantable.

03
Investor concentration

Over 35% non-owner-occupied is a flag. Over 50% renters is typically a full disqualifier.

04
Single-entity ownership

>10% of units in a 21+ unit project (20% in smaller). Usually a developer still holding inventory. Time-limited — know where you are in that curve.

05
Excessive commercial space

Over ~35% of square footage. DTLA, parts of Hollywood. Ground-floor retail is fine in moderation.

06
Hotel / transient operations

Short-term rentals at scale make Fannie treat the whole building as a hotel. Categorically ineligible.

07
Inadequate reserves

Below 10% today = already non-warrantable. Between 10% and 15% = a clock running out on Jan 4, 2027.

08
Unaddressed critical repairs

The rule from three slides ago. Funding is the way out.

09
SB 326 non-compliance

Missing balcony inspection on a wood-framed building. Increasingly treated as material.

STEP 02  ·  CLEAR — THE 15-ITEM CHECKLIST

A few phone calls. A whole escrow saved.

DOCUMENTS

6
  • HOA financials — current + prior year
  • Reserve study — under 30% funded is serious
  • Meeting minutes — last 12–24 months
  • Master insurance policy + fidelity bond
  • CC&Rs and bylaws
  • SB 326 report — the report, not a summary

QUESTIONS

5
  • Any pending or active litigation?
  • Any special assessment planned?
  • Current owner-occupancy ratio?
  • % of owners 60+ days delinquent? (15% cap)
  • Short-term rentals — allowed? enforced?

CONDITIONS

4
  • No single entity over 10% of units
  • Commercial space within limits
  • Reserves at or near the 15% threshold
  • Run the project status check. This is the one that catches what the other fourteen miss.

Sellers: start two weeks before listing — HOA management is the bottleneck.
Buyers: the same fifteen items, run before you write.
