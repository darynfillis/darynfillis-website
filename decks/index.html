What actually works up there

You're better at this than you think.

You ran companies in two countries. You've sat across from boards, buyers, and bankers. A room of agents is not the hard part — the hard part is trusting that you already know what to say.

01

The first ten seconds are the whole thing.

Don't thank them. Don't say "a little about myself." Don't apologize for the AV. Open cold, with the line that costs them something.

Every deck here opens with a hook for exactly this reason. Say it, then stop. The silence is doing more work than the next sentence would.

02

Silence is the most underused tool you own.

When a line lands, most people rush past it. Don't. Two seconds of nothing lets the room actually feel it.

It'll feel like an eternity from where you're standing. It's a beat from where they're sitting. Trust that gap.

03

Talk to one person at a time.

Not "the room." A room isn't listening — people are. Land a full thought on one face, then move to another.

It slows your pace naturally, kills the pacing-and-scanning habit, and makes twenty people each feel like you were talking to them.

04

Reveal one point at a time.

If the whole slide appears at once, they've read ahead and stopped listening. The click-reveals exist so you stay in control of the moment.

Say the thing, then reveal it. Not the other way around. The screen confirms you — it doesn't announce you.

05

Ask for something real.

Around the two-thirds mark, ask for hands. "Who has a condo listing right now?" Then actually wait. Count them out loud.

It flips a lecture into a conversation, and it tells you who to find at the coffee table afterward.

06

Nerves and excitement feel identical.

Same racing heart, same hands. The only difference is the word you put on it. Call it excitement. Your body can't tell the difference.

And the nerves usually mean you care about getting it right. That's not a flaw to fix — it's the reason you're good at this.

07

Make one clear ask. Then be quiet.

One action. Stupidly easy. Say it, put it on the screen, and give them five full seconds to write it down.

Do not fill that silence with a joke. That silence is where the phone comes out of the pocket. Let it happen.

08

Get there early. Test the sound.

Plug in. Play something. Put the deck in fullscreen. Find where you'll stand. Fifteen minutes of setup buys you fifteen minutes of calm.

On Zoom: check "Share sound" when you share the tab, or nobody hears the lobby music.
